Outer Space Treaty1967
Article II states that outer space, including the Moon and other celestial bodies, is not subject to national appropriation by claim of sovereignty, by means of use or occupation, or by any other means.

Luxembourg's Space Resources Law2017
The Luxembourg Space Resources Law (enacted 20 July 2017) establishes that: 1. Space resources (e.g., minerals, water) are capable of being owned, granting private companies property rights over resources they extract. 2. Ownership is recognized only after the entity obtains a specific authorization/licence from the competent minister for each exploration or utilization mission. 3. The law creates a licensing and supervision regime for space‑resource activities, separate from launch and registration rules. 4. It distinguishes between the appropriation of a celestial body (prohibited) and the appropriation of extracted material (permitted), aligning with the Outer Space Treaty. 5. It provides legal certainty for companies incorporated under Luxembourg law (or EU companies with a Luxembourg office) to claim ownership of extracted resources. 6. The framework includes provisions for liability, supervision, and tax incentives to attract space‑mining enterprises.
